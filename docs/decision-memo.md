# Household decision memo — 15 September 2026

The practical next decision is **when this household first needs the FI money**, not whether it can contribute for three or five years. A five-year contribution window can be followed by many years of holding; a house or car purchase in the next five years has a genuinely short horizon. The SEC cautions against relying on risky investments for money needed within five years. Keep emergency reserves and dated house/car money outside the FI investment pot, with a reserve sized to actual needs and held in the currency in which it will be spent. The dashboard's $25,000 reserve is merely an example, and it does **not** establish what the household's house and car will cost. [SEC: risk and horizon](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/gauge-your-risk-tolerance); [FDIC: insured deposits](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/brochures/insured-deposits).

**The U.S. is not obviously the superior place to concentrate every rupee of wealth.** A U.S. tax resident may find U.S.-domiciled broad funds convenient, but the household's spending currency, tax residence, retirement-account access, and future move matter. U.S. equities, Indian assets, and USD/INR each carry different risks. A stronger dollar in INR is possible, not assured; dollar-denominated FI assets do not hedge an Indian purchase perfectly. Vanguard argues that global diversification addresses the unknowable relative performance of U.S. and non-U.S. stocks, and the SEC explains how exchange-rate changes can help or hurt returns. [Vanguard: global diversification](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/think-differently-about-global-diversification.html); [SEC: currency risk](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/international-investing).

For a **long FI horizon and a tolerable temporary loss**, use a broad, inexpensive index as the stock core and choose the **stock/defensive split first**. The current dashboard illustrates **60% stocks / 40% defensive**, with the stock side shown as **45% VTI-style broad U.S. stocks and 15% VXUS-style broad international stocks**. The 40% defensive side can include appropriately chosen high-quality bonds and liquid assets; it does not promise principal safety because bond funds can fall too. This 60/40 mix is an editable illustration, **not yet a personally recommended allocation**. Given the household's anxiety about losses, its real loss tolerance and first withdrawal date could justify a lower stock share. The broader [investing framework](./investing-framework.md) gives a more cautious 50/40/10 discussion example under explicit long-horizon conditions. [SEC: allocation by horizon and tolerance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation); [SEC: bond-fund risks](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/bond-funds-and-income-funds).

The Nasdaq-100 fund QQQM is an **optional tilt**, default **0%** and capped at **10% of the whole investment pot** in the dashboard. If used, it replaces part of the broad U.S. stock sleeve rather than being added on top. It overlaps with big companies already owned through a broad U.S. index; separately buying the Magnificent Seven would compound that concentration. Invesco identifies the Nasdaq-100 as a non-diversified, more volatile exposure. No index, even a broad one, is guaranteed to be safe over a five-year period. [Invesco: QQQ holdings and risks](https://www.invesco.com/qqq-etf/en/about.html); [S&P: large-company overlap](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/equity/sp-500/).

## What the dashboard's default numbers actually say

These are **model outputs, not forecasts or actual balances**. Assume a possible $100,000 in June 2027, minus an illustrative $25,000 reserve, leaving **$75,000 investable**; add **$3,000 at each month-end for five years** ($180,000 more); assume **6% effective annual growth** converted to monthly compounding, **₹85 per USD**, no FX drift, and **5% annual inflation**. The ₹5 crore FI goal means **today's buying power as of 15 September 2026**, so its future nominal target is raised for inflation from today through the start date and the holding period. Returns, inflation, FX and taxes will differ in reality. Calculations use the dashboard model's month-end-contribution convention, checked independently.

The five-year pot consists of **$255,000 deposited** plus modeled growth; the extra five holding years increase it under the assumed return, but the inflation-adjusted target also grows. Extending holding time helps, yet it does not close this example's gap by itself. The required-monthly figures answer *what payment would mathematically close this modeled gap*; they are not an instruction to commit nearly $10,000 per month. If that payment is unrealistic, revisit the FI date, goal size, savings capacity, spending needs, and risk mix instead of simply assuming a higher return. The assumed $100,000 is **not a recorded account balance**, and the separate house/car goals are **not funded or priced** in this table.

The $75,000 long-horizon portion could be invested at once after reserves are assigned, or by a brief fixed schedule if a sharp early loss would derail the household's plan. Historically, Vanguard found immediate investment beat three-month staging roughly two-thirds of the time, while acknowledging the psychological value of short staging for a strongly loss-averse investor. That study does not tell us what June 2027 will do. Recent QQQ or Magnificent Seven returns and current valuations are not reliable buy dates. [Vanguard: lump sum versus staging](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf); [SEC: performance claims](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-47).

**FI corpus is not daily income.** A one-day 1% gain on ₹1 crore is ₹1 lakh of changing market value, not a repeatable cash payment; a 1% loss reverses it. The first withdrawal date, real spending, taxes, inflation, and bad early returns determine whether a corpus can support FI. Keep vested 401(k) balances visible in net worth but separate from easily accessible FI money: an early taxable distribution can face an additional tax unless an exception applies. U.S. tax residents generally report worldwide income, and moving to India or holding India-domiciled funds raises account-specific tax and reporting questions. Confirm residence status and account access before treating any cross-border holding as spendable. [Vanguard: retirement market and withdrawal risk](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/retirement/risks-you-face-in-retirement); [IRS: early distributions](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc558); [IRS: U.S. tax residence](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519); [IRS: foreign-fund reporting](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8621).

To personalize the plan, record actual savings and 401(k) balances, intended house/car dates and prices, first FI withdrawal date, required monthly FI spending in INR, debt, tax residence and likely move date, and the dollar loss the household could watch without needing to sell. Those answers determine whether the illustrated allocation and goal are even suitable. Weekly paper entries can test the habit while real balances remain separate.
