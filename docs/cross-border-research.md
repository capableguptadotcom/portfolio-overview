# U.S.–India investing questions for the dashboard

Research checked **15 September 2026** against the primary sources linked below. The working scenario is a person who is a **U.S. income-tax resident when investing begins in June 2027**, invests for **3–5 years**, and **might later move to India**. Citizenship, green-card history, account type, Indian tax residence, and U.S. estate-tax domicile are unspecified. Rules and broker policies should be checked again before investing and before any move. This is issue spotting, not a personal tax conclusion.

## Status changes the answer

- A U.S. **resident alien** is generally taxed on worldwide income like a U.S. citizen. Residence may arise under the substantial-presence or green-card tests; a visa label alone does not settle it. U.S. citizens generally remain subject to U.S. worldwide-income reporting after moving abroad, and a green-card holder can remain a resident alien until status ends under the applicable rules. [IRS Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519), [IRS substantial-presence test](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/substantial-presence-test), [IRS immigration and tax status](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/taxation-of-aliens-by-visa-type-and-immigration-status).
- Indian income-tax residence is determined **each tax year**. India distinguishes nonresident, resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR), and resident and ordinarily resident (ROR); ROR generally includes foreign-source income, while RNOR has a narrower foreign-income scope. A return to India does not immediately establish a single enduring tax treatment. [India Income Tax Department: residential status](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/residential-status).
- U.S. **estate-tax residence** depends on domicile and can differ from U.S. income-tax residence. An intention to move permanently matters, so a planned return to India and eventual facts cannot be converted into an estate-tax status now. [IRS Form 706-NA instructions, definitions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na), [IRS Internal Revenue Manual: domicile](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-025-004).

## Holdings and taxes

- U.S. persons holding a foreign corporation that meets the **PFIC** passive-income (75%) or passive-asset (50%) test can face Form 8621 reporting and special tax rules. An Indian mutual fund or ETF may be within this regime; its legal structure and facts must be checked before purchase. Direct shares of an operating Indian company are **not automatically PFICs** merely because the issuer is foreign, but the company itself can qualify if it meets a test. Do not present Indian funds and direct shares as tax-equivalent choices. [IRS Form 8621 instructions, PFIC tests](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8621), [IRS Form 8621 overview](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621).
- U.S. foreign tax credit is **not blanket protection against double taxation**. The foreign tax must qualify, and Form 1116 generally limits the credit to U.S. tax attributable to foreign-source taxable income in the relevant income category. Income source, timing, treaty rules, and whether the same income was taxed in each country matter. India also has a rule-based credit for residents when corresponding foreign-taxed income is offered to Indian tax. [IRS qualifying foreign taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-taxes-that-qualify-for-the-foreign-tax-credit), [IRS Publication 514, limitations](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p514), [India Rule 128](https://wmstatic-prd.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/20117/42998/Rule-128_2026-01-13_11-37-01_1c629b_en.pdf/145a3343-3b83-7223-7064-8fd9194f161c?download=true&t=1775731788761&version=6.0).
- A U.S. person with Indian bank or brokerage accounts may have **separate account-reporting duties**: FBAR if aggregate foreign-account value exceeds $10,000 at any point in a calendar year, and Form 8938 if its applicable threshold is exceeded. These are reporting thresholds, not investment limits. [IRS FBAR guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar), [IRS Form 8938 overview](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938).

## 401(k) and a later move

- A 401(k) balance belongs in **net worth** but is not the same as immediately spendable brokerage cash. Plan documents control when distributions are available. Most taxable distributions before age **59½** face a **10% additional U.S. tax** unless an exception applies; ordinary income tax may also apply. Moving abroad alone is not listed as an early-distribution exception. [IRS hardship/withdrawal/loan guidance](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/hardships-early-withdrawals-and-loans), [IRS Topic 558](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc558).
- If the payee later becomes a **foreign person**, U.S.-source qualified-plan distributions generally trigger **30% withholding** absent reliable status and treaty documentation supporting a lower rate. The U.S.–India income-tax treaty's private-pension article defines a pension as a **periodic payment**; the treaty technical explanation says a single lump sum does not qualify under that article. Distribution form, payee status, and treaty application therefore need individual review. [IRS plan distributions to foreign persons](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-distributions-to-foreign-persons-require-withholding), [U.S.–India income-tax treaty, Article 20](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/india.pdf), [Treasury technical explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/inditech.pdf).

## Schwab access and tax forms

- Schwab says its international account can trade U.S. stocks, ETFs, and other products for **eligible** international investors, and that opening depends on the **country of residence**. It provides an account-conversion form and a client-information update form. These public pages do **not** establish that an India resident can open or retain every specific Schwab account, or buy every U.S. ETF after moving. Confirm the actual account, India eligibility, allowed holdings and new purchases with Schwab before treating access as a planning assumption. Schwab's published U.S.-ETF purchase restriction explicitly concerns **EEA/UK** residents; it should not be generalized to India. [Schwab account opening](https://international.schwab.com/open-account-intro), [Schwab International account](https://international.schwab.com/us-investing-brokerage-account), [Schwab forms](https://international.schwab.com/forms-and-applications), [Schwab ETF restriction](https://international.schwab.com/investment-products/etf-trading/how-do-etfs-work).
- **W-9 versus W-8BEN follows U.S. tax status, not passport or mailing address alone.** A U.S. citizen or resident alien uses W-9; a nonresident alien beneficial owner generally uses W-8BEN. IRS instructions require notification to the payer within **30 days** after becoming a U.S. citizen or resident alien following a W-8BEN; later status changes must be documented correctly with the payer rather than assumed from a move. [IRS W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben), [IRS beneficial-owner documentation](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/beneficial-owners).

## Estate exposure after leaving the U.S.

- For a person who is **neither a U.S. citizen nor U.S.-domiciled at death**, U.S. estate tax generally reaches U.S.-situated assets. Stock of a corporation organized under U.S. law is generally U.S.-situated even when held through a broker abroad. Form 706-NA has a **$60,000 U.S.-situated-asset filing threshold** (with specified gift adjustments); this is **not** a universal tax-free investment allowance. A U.S.-domiciled noncitizen or U.S. citizen falls into a different worldwide-estate framework. Do not assume moving to India removes exposure from U.S.-domiciled ETFs or stocks; confirm product structure and estate status separately. [IRS Form 706-NA instructions: domicile, situs, and filing](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na), [IRS estate-tax overview](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-tax-for-nonresidents-not-citizens-of-the-united-states).
- The U.S.–India treaty linked by the IRS is an **income-tax treaty**. India does not appear in the IRS Form 706 instructions' list of estate/death-tax conventions. The income-tax treaty should therefore not be presented as automatic estate-tax relief. [IRS India treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/india-tax-treaty-documents), [IRS Form 706 instructions, death-tax treaties](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706).

## Dashboard implications

1. Keep **net worth**, **liquid investing money**, and **401(k)/restricted retirement assets** separate. A 3–5-year goal should not assume penalty-free retirement withdrawals.
2. Record **tax citizenship**, current U.S. income-tax status, Indian tax status by year, and prospective U.S. estate domicile as **unknown or scenario inputs**, never inferred from a single address field.
3. Mark Indian mutual funds/ETFs as **PFIC-review-required** during U.S.-person years; distinguish direct operating-company shares without promising they are PFIC-free.
4. Show tax estimates as **illustrative gross values** unless asset-specific U.S./Indian tax, credits, source, and timing have been reviewed. A single “tax treaty removes double tax” assumption would be misleading.
5. Add a **pre-move review checkpoint** for Schwab account/product permissions, W-9/W-8BEN status, 401(k) distribution strategy, India RNOR/ROR timing, and U.S.-situs estate exposure.
